## Objective

Test whether project-based services firms encounter a repeated, consequential project-acceptance or reshaping decision that their current process and configured CRM/PSA/ERP do not adequately support. Explore whether time-phased capacity, finance-approved project economics, scenario comparison, and cash timing matter to that specific decision. Do not pitch a solution or treat positive reactions as product validation.

The Software & IT Services segment, buyer, workflow, and proposed human decision brief remain hypotheses. The public-source findings and limits are recorded in the competitive-validation report.

## Authorized scope and limits

### In scope under the approved protocol

- Non-leading interviews about a participant’s most recent real decision to accept, reject, delay, phase, reprice, outsource, or staff a project.
- Understanding how the participant used current systems, spreadsheets, people, and policies; which evidence was trusted; and what happened after the decision.
- A written, evidence-tagged synthesis that separates participant reports, vendor documentation/marketing, customer stories, and researcher inference.

### Not authorized

- Any product implementation, code, connector, prototype, or Product Baseline change.
- Access to customer systems, credentials, live records, or confidential datasets; importing or processing customer data.
- Creating vendor trial/demo accounts, paid trials, subscriptions, or other spend. Any such step requires separate explicit approval. No vendor outreach or demo booking is planned in this protocol as currently authorized.
- Audio/video recording or collection of identifiable/confidential business information. Before first outreach, use the consent script below and choose an access-controlled location and retention/deletion period for minimal anonymized notes. This protocol does not select that private location or period; keep raw notes and personal contact details out of this repository.

## Participant profile for the exploratory sample

The founder selected an exploratory sample across these roles:

- Owner/CEO/COO or delivery leader accountable for project acceptance and delivery risk.
- Finance lead/controller responsible for cost, margin, billing, receivables, and cash forecasts.
- Resource/staffing lead responsible for skills, capacity, availability, and hiring/subcontracting options.
- Sales/BD leader who prepares bids/proposals and hands won work to delivery.

Prefer people who personally participated in a recent decision and can describe their actual process. The founder will recruit and conduct **9–12 interviews** across these roles; no quota by role is set. Company size and geography are not selection criteria yet because the ICP remains unvalidated; capture broad, non-identifying bands only where useful. Do not assume every role or company fits the eventual ICP.

## Interview guide

**Opening script:** “I’m researching how project-services teams make decisions about new work. Taking part is voluntary. I will take anonymized notes only—no audio/video recording and no identifying quotations. Please do not share client/project names, exact commercial figures, credentials, or confidential records; you may skip any question or stop at any time. Is it okay to proceed with anonymized notes?” Proceed only after agreement.

Start with a recent event rather than hypothetical feature requests. Do not reveal the desired product answer before the participant describes their current process.

1. Walk through the most recent project/tender that your team accepted, declined, delayed, or reshaped. What was the decision, when was it made, and who owned it?
2. What alternatives did you consider (for example, current team, subcontracting, hiring, phasing, repricing, postponing, or declining)? What evidence changed the decision?
3. What did you know about demand, skills, time-phased capacity, delivery dates, price, direct cost, margin, billing milestones, receivables, and expected cash timing? Which systems, spreadsheets, or people supplied each item?
4. Which inputs were missing, stale, disputed, or difficult to combine? Who resolved the uncertainty, and how long did the process take?
5. What was the consequence of being wrong or late? Ask for a concrete observed consequence; do not assume that a reported benefit was caused by a particular tool.
6. What can your current CRM/PSA/ERP already do for quote estimates, capacity, margin, scenario comparison, invoice forecasts, and expected payment dates? What did you configure, trial, reject, or work around?
7. When you say “cash pressure,” do you mean planned invoicing, payment terms/expected dates, collection history, probability of payment, working capital, or available cash? Which data is authoritative and who may use it?
8. How was the final decision recorded, approved, and communicated? What action followed, who executed it, and how did you verify the outcome?
9. What would make you trust a separate workflow enough to evaluate or buy it? What budget, authority, procurement, security, or integration barrier applies? What concrete next step would you take if the need were real?

Use neutral follow-ups: “What happened next?”, “How do you know?”, “Can you give a recent example?”, “Which system showed that?”, “What did you do when the answer was missing?” Separate observed behavior from opinions and hypothetical intent.

## Evidence capture and synthesis

Under the approved anonymized-notes-only protocol, the founder may keep minimal notes in an approved, access-controlled location. Do not store raw notes in this repository. Keep names, contact details, customer/project names, exact commercial figures, credentials, and copied source records out of this workspace.

Suggested fields:

- Participant code (not name), role, broad organization-size/industry band, date, and interviewer.
- Decision episode and recency; alternatives actually considered; decision owner and steps.
- Input categories and source systems; freshness, uncertainty, and reconciliation steps.
- Time/effort and specific consequence described; participant’s own attribution versus observed fact.
- Current incumbent features/configuration, workaround, and reason for any gap.
- Cash concept discussed (invoice forecast, expected payment date, collection history/probability, working capital, or available cash).
- Paraphrase only under current authorization; no direct or identifying quotations.
- Evidence tag: **[I]** participant report, **[D]** official documentation, **[M]** vendor claim, **[C]** vendor-published customer story, **[B]** benchmark, **[NR]** not established in reviewed sources, or **[A]** analyst inference.
- Open question, contradiction, and follow-up required.

Do not treat a single anecdote, vendor case study, public benchmark, positive reaction, or demo as proof of market demand or causal value.

## Evaluation gate — exploratory only

The founder selected exploratory discovery with no numeric pass/fail threshold. Do not treat 9–12 interviews as a representative market sample or claim product validation. Summarize supporting and disconfirming evidence against these questions:

- Whether participants can describe repeated, recent decisions with decision owners and real consequences.
- Whether a specific decision-critical failure persists after considering the incumbent’s existing capabilities and configuration.
- Whether required inputs have authoritative, timely sources and can be used under the customer’s governance/security constraints.
- Whether participants distinguish cash concepts and can explain which one changes the decision.
- Whether a concrete buyer, budget/process, and next commercial commitment exists—not just feature interest.

At the end, report supporting and disconfirming evidence, sample limitations, unresolved contradictions, incumbent coverage, and a recommendation. Do not label the product validated or authorize an MVP based on discovery alone.
